Storing Pinot Gris

Storing Pinot Gris wine is important in order to ensure that it stays fresh and flavorful for as long as possible. Pinot Gris is an aromatic white wine that is best enjoyed within a few years of production, as it does not have the structure or tannins to age for long periods of time like some fuller-bodied red wines.

As a general rule, light and aromatic Pinot Gris should be consumed within one to two years of production, while oaked Pinot Grigio can last for three to four years. It is important to store Pinot Gris in a dark, climate-controlled environment at a consistent temperature between 12°C and 18°C. Avoid exposing the wine to sunlight or large temperature fluctuations, as these can negatively affect the flavor and quality of the wine.

If your Pinot Gris is sealed with a cork, it is best to store it on its side to keep the cork moist and prevent air from entering the bottle. If the wine is in a bottle with a screw top, it does not matter whether you store it on its side or not.

Once you have opened a bottle of Pinot Gris, it is important to consume it within five days to ensure that it stays fresh and flavorful. If you are unsure whether your Pinot Gris has gone bad, trust your eyes and nose. A yellowish color or funky, musty aroma may indicate that the wine has gone off.

Serving Pinot Gris

Serving New Zealand Pinot Gris at the right temperature and in the right glassware can greatly enhance the flavor and enjoyment of the wine. Pinot Gris is an aromatic white wine that is best enjoyed when chilled, but not too cold. A temperature of around 7°C is ideal for most Pinot Gris, as it allows the flavors and aromas to be fully expressed. If the wine is too cold, it may lose some of its flavor and aroma.

In terms of glassware, Pinot Gris is best served in a traditional white wine glass. These glasses are wider than champagne flutes but narrower than large red wine glasses, and they help to focus the flavors of the wine on the center of the tongue rather than the sides, where the acidity is more pronounced. The tapering around the rim of the glass also helps to hold in the aromas of the wine, allowing you to fully appreciate the flavors and aromas of the Pinot Gris.

By following these simple guidelines, you can ensure that your New Zealand Pinot Gris is served at its best, allowing you to fully enjoy the flavors and aromas of this versatile and aromatic white wine.

Pairing Pinot Gris

Pinot Gris is a versatile white wine that pairs well with a wide range of foods. It is particularly well suited to seafood, and is a great choice for pairing with sushi or other dishes featuring salmon or tuna. The crisp acidity of Pinot Gris helps to cut through the richness of these flavors, making for a delicious and harmonious pairing.

Pinot Grigio cheese pairing

Pinot Gris is also a good choice for pairing with mild, soft cheeses such as brie, camembert, or mozzarella. These cheeses are relatively mild in flavor, and their earthy, mineral notes complement the flavors of Pinot Gris nicely.

Pairing Pinot Gris with desserts

When it comes to dessert, Pinot Gris is best paired with relatively light, not-too-sweet options such as pavlova, crème brûlée, or fruit-filled crepes. The dryness of Pinot Gris can be overwhelmed by overly sweet desserts, so it is best to choose something that is not too rich or cloying.

Pinot Grigio with lamb

Pinot Gris is not generally the best choice for pairing with lamb, as the richness of the meat tends to overpower the flavors of the wine. Instead, try a Pinot Noir for a tender young lamb or a Syrah for grilled lamb chops.

Pinot Grigio for cooking

Pinot Gris is also a good choice for cooking, as it is relatively neutral in flavor and unlikely to overpower other ingredients in a dish. It is particularly good for adding to cream sauces or seafood chowders, as its acidity helps to cut through the richness of these dishes.

If you have an open bottle of Pinot Gris in the fridge and are looking for other ways to use it, consider adding it to a vinaigrette or marinade for grilled chicken or fish. Its acidity and fruity flavors can help to brighten up the flavors of these dishes.

Pinot Grigio with light meals

Pinot Gris is also a good choice for pairing with lighter, vegetarian dishes such as salads, grilled vegetables, or tofu. Its crisp acidity and light body make it a refreshing and complementing choice for these types of dishes.

Overall, Pinot Gris is a versatile and food-friendly wine that pairs well with a wide range of dishes. Whether you are looking for a wine to enjoy with sushi, cheese, dessert, or a main course, Pinot Gris is a great choice that is sure to please.
